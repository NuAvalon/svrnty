# Manifesto — Tiny Landlords

**You are the landlord.**

Not of a platform. Of *your* relationships, *your* keys, *your* projects, *your* agents.
The important copy lives on your device (and on sealed stores you unlock).
Everyone else in the stack is plumbing — or a guest you admitted.

---

## The old story

For twenty years “the cloud” meant one big landlord:

- Your contacts sat in their database  
- Your messages crossed their servers  
- Your identity was a row they could freeze  
- Your collaborators were whoever their account system allowed  

They called it a network. It was a tenancy. Convenience was the rent.
When the landlord changed the rules — or got breached, bored, or bought — you noticed you never owned the house.

Trust that depends on a third party isn’t trust. It’s permission.

---

## The svrnty story

**A bunch of tiny landlords, working together.**

Each person (and each agent they vouch for) is sovereign over their own graph.
We meet by ceremony and keys — not by uploading our address books to a mutual overseer.
We speak through **dumb, interchangeable pipes** that are not allowed to read what they carry.

`svrnty.is` is not your landlord. It is:

- an **open-source PWA** you can run yourself  
- **default plumbing** (relay, nursery, docs) you can replace  
- a **rendezvous**, never a source of truth for your relationships  

Leave, self-host, friend-host, rent a container, move house.
If the important bits don’t come with you, we failed.

---

## What “landlord” means here

| Role | Who | Power |
|------|-----|--------|
| **Landlord** | You (the keyholder) | Admit, revoke, export, delete, read your own sealed stores |
| **Guest** | A human or agent you’ve added | May speak to you; may be removed |
| **Pipe** | Relay / container host | Hold opaque bytes; get replaced; never entitled to plaintext |
| **Big landlord** | The model we refuse | Read the graph, trap the address, revoke the tenancy |

Hosts may collect rent for electricity and uptime.
They do not collect your relationships.

---

## Rules we build under

1. **Local first.** Keys and the living book live with you.  
2. **Admit to speak.** Strangers don’t get a slot until you add their key. Anti-spam is structural.  
3. **Blind pipes.** Relays and containers see ciphertext (and as little metadata as we can manage).  
4. **Interchangeable plumbing.** Default `svrnty.is` must be swappable without losing the graph.  
5. **Humans and agents are peers** under the same admit rules — different kinds of guest, same citizenship test.  
6. **Export is a ritual, not a support ticket.** Leaving proves sovereignty.  
7. **Honest claims.** We don’t say “messaging” until the crypto matches; we don’t say “the host can’t read it” if it can.

## Why this matters for projects

Cross-org (and cross-trust) work — humans and agents, async, on shared projects — usually recreates a big landlord so everyone has a shared room.

We want the other shape:

- each tiny landlord keeps their vault  
- a project is a **sealed ring of admitted members**, not a server-side roster  
- agents act as contacts you’ve let in; their work can land in *your* sealed archive  
- the pipe that moves updates stays dumb  

Collaboration UIs in the wider **svrnty ecosystem** (separate projects that speak this substrate) can sit on top of that physics.
They are not this repository — and they don’t need a readable landlord either.

**Many landlords coordinating without surrendering the deed.**

## The test

If we disappear tomorrow and you still have:

- your keys  
- your book  
- your projects’ capability to move to another pipe  

…then the model held.

If your collab dies with our domain name, we became what we set out to replace.
